Principles behind this plan

Four rules shape every choice below.

A community is a product

Grow it so members and the company win together. Track how people join, stay, and take part.

Design from the member's side

Every choice answers one question: would someone want to join, and stay?

Follow the data

Watch the trend. Spot the pattern. Fix what's broken.

Grow members, not just the company

Success means members grow and their circles grow, not only Neander's numbers.

01

Where Neander fits

In 2026 the market split into two camps. Both leave the same gap open. Neander sits in that gap: take an idea to a real, live product without starting over. And it's built for a team, not one person.

fig.01 — the gap no one owns

So we recruit team builders who feel the rebuild pain. Not hobbyists chasing a quick demo. Wrong crowd = wrong feedback.

02

Show the problem first

People don't look for a fix until they feel the problem. Most of the market doesn't feel it yet. So we help them see it, one step at a time. Name it. Prove it. Then show Neander. We never lead with the product.

fig.02 — five steps, from "I'm fine" to "I build in Neander"

Each post has one job: move someone up one step. Start with their problem, in their words.

Step 1 · name it

Give the pain a simple name people repeat — like "the rebuild tax." Owning the name helps us get found later, in search and in AI answers.

Step 2 · prove it

Show real breakdowns: "we built X fast, here's what broke when it went live." They see themselves in it.

Step 3 · show the fix

Only once they feel the pain: show how Neander keeps the first build and grows it. No throwaway work.

03

Recruit and run: focus groups + hands-on sessions

Pre-launch, we don't have the spotlight to make a "closed group" feel special. So we skip the tiers. We recruit users where they already are — meetups, focus groups, small rooms — and we get feedback the honest way: put the tool in front of them and build together. One group, real work, real people.

fig.03 — how a user gets from a meetup to feedback we can use

One rule: no feedback without hands on the tool. Opinions before use are noise; behaviour during use is signal.

Where we find them

Same rooms as before, but with a clear ask: after the meetup, invite 3–5 promising people to a build session.

The build session — the core unit

This is where feedback comes from. Two formats, same idea.

In-person · Bangalore first

Hands-on build night

6–10 people, one evening. Everyone brings a real thing they want to build. Team helps unblock. We watch where they get stuck. Ends with a group debrief.

Why: body language and side chatter tell us more than any survey.

Online · both cities

Multiplayer build session in the tool

2–4 people in one Neander project, live. A team member co-builds. Screen shared. We capture friction as it happens.

Why: this uses the multiplayer feature itself — we're testing the product with the product.

How we capture feedback

A simple, repeatable system. Same fields every time so patterns show up across sessions.

FieldWhat we log
WhoName, role, team size, what they were building
Stuck pointsEvery place they slowed down, got confused, or asked for help
Words usedThe exact phrases they used to describe the problem (fuel for the "name the pain" work in §02)
What they lovedMoments where the tool clicked
Rebuild score1–5: how close is this to something they'd take to production?
Next stepFollow-up ask, another session, or a case study

All of it lands in one shared board tagged by theme. When three sessions flag the same friction, it goes to the roadmap that week.

04

First 90 days, then the long game

Two timelines, one system. The first 90 days build the feedback engine that de-risks v2. After that, the same engine grows on its own — members start bringing Neander to others.

Short term · 0–90 days

The feedback engine

goal: v2 shaped by real users, ready to launch with proof
  • Name the problem, tell the story
  • Recruit users from meetups and focus groups
  • Run build sessions every week (in-person + online)
  • Log every session, act on the patterns
  • Collect real quotes and first case studies
Long term · 90 days–12 months+

The loop that grows itself

goal: members bring Neander to others
  • Get into colleges and startup programs
  • Turn session builds into search + AI traffic
  • Open the public community at v2 launch
  • Add an expert/agency layer after launch
  • Standing build-night series in both cities

Long term — three bets that build on each other

Bet 1 · reach future founders

Colleges + startup programs

Many future founders are in business schools and campus startup programs right now. Get in early. When they start up, Neander is already their tool. They take it to market for us.

Start: NSRCEL's Campus Founders at IIM Bangalore already helps students go "prototype to growth" — a clean fit. Then more campus clubs. SF later (Berkeley SkyDeck, Stanford StartX).

How: build sprints, free credits, a campus lead, hackathons.

Bet 2 · get found

Member content → search + AI

Every build session, story, and case study becomes a page. Those pages rank in Google and get quoted by AI tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity. Nearly half of business buyers now use AI to research tools.

Own the name: when someone (or an AI) searches the pain, our name and our builds are the answer. Good AI-friendly content can lift how often you get quoted by up to ~40% (KDD 2024 study).

How: members re-share their builds, and a public gallery holds them all.

Bet 3 · grow the people

Experts + agencies (post-launch)

After v2, add an expert/agency layer so builders earn money doing Neander work. Bigger teams trust a tool more when independent experts back it. The best users from our build sessions are the first names on that list.

Why it works: the member grows their own business with Neander. Their win and our win are the same win.

06

Track the loop, not the crowd

The goal is good feedback and steady growth, not big numbers for show. So we watch two scorecards, on purpose: ours and the member's. If members aren't growing, the engine is broken — no matter what our own numbers say.

07

Things to watch

Fake quotes on the site. The current testimonials mention "Synergy™/Apex™" and expense-tracking copy that has nothing to do with the product. Sharp early users notice right away. First job: get real member quotes up.

Wrong crowd. Hobbyist channels bring hobbyist feedback. Focus on team builders who feel the go-live pain.

Sessions without capture. A great build night with no notes is a wasted night. Every session needs the shared board filled in, or the pattern never shows up.

Ignoring feedback. A feedback group that never acts on feedback slowly empties out. "You asked, we shipped" keeps people around.

One-person risk. Write the playbook down so the engine survives launch, growth, and handoff.
