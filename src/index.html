How I work

I treat community as a growth channel, not a cost center.

Every Reddit post, creator video, and ambassador-shipped tutorial is a long-tail surface that compounds. At Firecrawl, I reframed program KPIs from lagging signups to leading SEO/GEO impressions — because the lagging metrics couldn't tell us what to do next week, and the leading ones could.

I build the tooling instead of waiting for it.

TubeMonitor is the recent example, but the pattern is older — the Apps Script automations at Google, the Notion stacks, the dashboards that actually answer the question. AI tooling has changed what one operator can ship solo, and I lean into that hard. I'm not a production engineer; I orchestrate AI tooling like a PM directs an engineering team.

I pick the bar and hold it.

The hardest part of running an ambassador program isn't recruiting — it's saying no to the people who don't meet the bar, defending that decision, and watching the program get stronger because of it. I've designed those bars at three different companies. They've held.

I'm hands-on. I write the briefs, manage the agencies, and ship the artifacts.

If you're looking for someone to set strategy and hand off execution, I'm not it. I work better the closer I am to the actual surface — the Reddit thread, the creator brief, the milestone payout. Strategy without execution is the failure mode I've seen most often kill community programs.

What I'm not the right person for

  • —Pure event management — I can run an event, but it's not my edge. If the role is mostly logistics for conferences and meetups, hire someone else.
  • —Writing technical tutorials yourself — I'm a content operator, not a writer. I direct creators and agencies; I don't sit down and draft "How to use X with Y."
  • —Outbound sales / BDR work — Not my zone.
  • —Production engineering — I orchestrate AI tooling to ship internal apps. I don't ship customer-facing code.
  • —Performance marketing or paid ads — Different muscle, not mine.
